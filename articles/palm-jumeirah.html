Palm Jumeirah — Artificial Coastlines, Dredging and Environmental Trade-Offs

Palm Jumeirah is a major example of humans deliberately changing a coastal landscape. The project uses dredging and reclamation to create new land and coastline, showing how economic goals, engineering and environmental management can interact.

Why build an artificial coastline?

Dubai sought to expand tourism and property development as part of economic diversification beyond oil. Creating artificial islands increased waterfront land, which has high commercial and residential value.

The palm shape is therefore both an engineering design and an economic strategy.

Dredging and reclamation

Dredging removes sediment from the seabed. That material is then transported and placed to create new land. This directly modifies the lithosphere and seabed habitat.

Reclamation can also increase turbidity, bury benthic organisms and change the way currents move sediment.

The breakwater and water circulation

The crescent around Palm Jumeirah acts as a breakwater, reducing wave energy that would otherwise damage the reclaimed island.

However, enclosing water can reduce circulation. Openings and engineering modifications are therefore used to improve exchange between the lagoon and the open sea.

Scale of the project

Project sources support roughly 110 million cubic metres of sand moved, around 700 hectares of new land and an approximately 11-kilometre protective crescent.

Palm Jumeirah itself added around 70–72 kilometres of coastline. This is a more appropriate Palm-Jumeirah-specific figure than the much larger number sometimes associated with Dubai’s wider artificial-island ambitions.

Environmental management questions

The project raises questions that do not have a single yes-or-no answer. New land and tourism generate economic benefits, but reclamation also changes habitat, water circulation, sediment transport and the visual landscape.

A strong geographical evaluation should therefore consider both benefits and costs, the groups who receive them and the effectiveness of mitigation.
