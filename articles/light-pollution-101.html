Artificial light: benefit and unintended consequence

Electric lighting has transformed safety, work, transport and recreation after dark. The environmental problem begins when light is brighter than necessary, aimed where it is not needed or left on without purpose.

That makes light pollution a design problem as well as an energy problem.

Glare, light trespass and skyglow

Glare is excessive brightness that makes seeing less comfortable or more difficult. Light trespass occurs when unwanted light enters another property or habitat. Skyglow is the brightening of the night sky over populated areas.

These categories are useful because they identify different management solutions. Shielding can reduce trespass and skyglow; lower brightness can reduce glare.

Wildlife impacts

The video uses sea turtles as an example of wildlife sensitivity. Hatchlings normally use natural light cues to orient toward the sea. Artificial coastal lighting can draw them in the wrong direction.

Other species can also be affected because feeding, migration, reproduction and predator-prey interactions have evolved around natural day-night cycles.

Human sleep and energy

Artificial light at night can interfere with sleep and circadian timing, especially when bright light reaches the eyes during biologically sensitive periods.

Unnecessary lighting also wastes electricity. Where electricity is generated from fossil fuels, wasted lighting can indirectly contribute to greenhouse-gas and air-pollution emissions.

Why light pollution keeps growing

The video connects increasing light pollution with urban growth, population, electrification and expanded infrastructure.

Management therefore includes better fixture design, lower intensity, warmer wavelengths where appropriate, timers, curfews and protection of sensitive habitats.
