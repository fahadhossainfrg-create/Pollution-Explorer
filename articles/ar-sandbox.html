Augmented Reality Sandbox — Making Topographic Maps Visible

The augmented-reality sandbox is a visual tool that turns a pile of real sand into a live topographic map. It is useful because it allows students to see how a three-dimensional landscape becomes two-dimensional elevation colours and contour lines.

How the system works

The sandbox combines physical sand, a depth-sensing camera, computer software and a projector. The camera measures the height of the sand surface. Software converts those height measurements into elevation information.

The projector then places colours and contour lines directly on top of the sand.

Changing the land changes the map

When a user builds a hill, digs a valley or creates a basin, the system updates instantly. The physical landform and the digital topographic representation remain connected.

This makes contour lines easier to understand because the student can create a slope and immediately see how the line spacing changes.

Contour spacing and slope

Contour lines join places of equal elevation. If elevation changes quickly over a short horizontal distance, the contour lines are close together and the slope is steep.

If elevation changes gradually, the contours are farther apart. The sandbox therefore creates a direct visual relationship between slope shape and map pattern.

Catchments and hydrology

Many AR sandbox systems can simulate water. Digital rainfall can flow downhill, collect in low areas and follow drainage paths.

That makes the tool useful for understanding catchments, watersheds, flood pathways and how topography influences the movement of water — all relevant to pollution runoff and coastal management.

Study and assessment questions

1. Why do steep slopes have closely spaced contour lines?
2. How does the system connect 3-D terrain to a 2-D map?
3. How could simulated rain show a catchment?
4. How could the sandbox be used to model pollution runoff?
